Research Interests

Machine learning automation of Molecular Beam Epitaxy (MBE) for topological quantum materials synthesis; nonlinear transport phenomena (nonlinear Hall effect, in-plane Hall effect, full-wave mixing); Kagome lattice compounds (FeSn, Fe₃Sn, Co₃Sn); topological antiferromagnets, Weyl semimetals and altermagnets.

Research Experience

Graduate Research Assistant 2021 – 2026
The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology · Advisor: Prof. Berthold Jäck
  • Commissioned, assembled, and maintained two AI-automated Molecular Beam Epitaxy (MBE) systems, leveraging Bayesian optimization-based machine learning algorithms to autonomously optimize thin-film growth.
  • Epitaxial thin-film synthesis of intermetallic Kagome lattice compounds (FeSn, Fe₃Sn, Co₃Sn) using Molecular Beam Epitaxy (MBE).
  • Micro- and nanofabrication: multi-step optical lithography for patterning Hall bar devices (6- and 12-terminal geometries) and cross Co-planar Wave Guides (CPW) geometries; Focused Ion Beam (FIB) milling for TEM lamella preparation and Hall device prototyping for antimonides (CrSb).
  • Structural and chemical characterization: Thin-film X-ray Diffraction (XRD), X-ray Reflectivity (XRR), and phi-scans for phase identification, thickness determination, and epitaxial relationship analysis.
  • Advanced microscopy: Scanning/Transmission Electron Microscopy (STEM/TEM) for atomic-scale structural and chemical analysis; In-situ Reflection High-Energy Electron Diffraction (RHEED) for real-time surface reconstruction and growth dynamics; Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM) for surface roughness measurements.
  • Magnetotransport measurements: Nonlinear electric Hall effect, in-plane electric Hall effect, and electric full-wave mixing measurements using oscilloscope-based techniques at low and room temperature.
  • Microwave measurements: Used a Vector Network Analyser (VNA) to test a 3rd-order Nonlinear Hall response in the range of (1–6 GHz) on a cross CPW transmission line.
💡

Notable Contributions

  • World's first experimental demonstration of the Berry curvature quadrupole — a fundamental quantum geometric quantity — in an antiferromagnetic material, confirming the theoretical prediction by Prof. Kam Tuen Law's group at HKUST, Hong Kong.
  • World's first experimental observation of the Berry curvature quadrupole in a g-wave altermagnet at room temperature, extending the study of quantum geometry to this emerging class, predicted by Prof. Jenifer Cano at University of Stony Brook, USA.
  • World's first demonstration of multiple wave mixing (third-order nonlinear transport) in a room-temperature altermagnet, uncovering a broadband nonlinear Hall response.
  • Molecular Beam Epitaxy growth and characterization of a previously unreported metastable Kagome compound, bilayer Co₃Sn, enabling exploration of its anomalous Hall response.
  • Implementation of active learning and machine learning algorithms to optimize molecular beam epitaxy growth, drastically reducing the number of experimental iterations required for synthesizing complex quantum materials.
